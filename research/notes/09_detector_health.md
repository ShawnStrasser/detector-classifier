# 09_detector_health — ODOT's production detector health, ported to a 3-day sample (2026-09-21)

Code `src/health2.py` (`find_bad_intervals` / `mask_intervals` / `trust_score` / `summarise` / `describe_intervals`) + drivers `src/health2_{build,features,mask_eval,trust}.py`; artefacts `dc_work/health2/`; ODOT-only pull script (git-ignored, **not run**) `src/download/health_pull.py`. DEV only, TEST never read. `src/predict.py` untouched — the exact edits it would need are specified in the `health2.py` docstring ("Integration into src/predict.py").

## 1. What `ATSPM_Report` does (read-only study, incl. `atspm` + `traffic_anomaly`)
Not a bespoke detector: a **volume-anomaly model on 15-min counts of event 82**. **Event 81 is never fetched**, so occupancy, ON duration and stuck-on are not observable in production at all. Pipeline: zero-filled 15-min bins per `(DeviceId, Detector)` (`fill_in_missing=True` + `known_detectors`, `max_days_old=7`) → `traffic_anomaly.decompose` over **35 days** with `rolling_window_enable=False`, i.e. the trend is a **static per-detector median** (`drop_days`, `min_rolling_window_samples` are inert) plus time-of-day and day-of-week seasonal medians, `min_time_of_day_samples=14` → `anomaly` = signed **GEH > 6.0** with under-counts amplified (`log_adjust_negative`) **AND** residual **> 3 SD from its peer group** (corridor `group_name`) at the same timestamp → daily `PercentAnomalous` → date-weighted CUSUM (k=1, 7 days) → alert when `CUSUM>0.20 & z>3.5 & PercentAnomalous>0.10` **on the most recent day**, suppressed 21 days per `(DeviceId, Detector)`; per-region PDF emailed 05:30 Mon–Fri, "Ongoing" section on Mondays. Worth copying: the **peer gate** (a corridor-wide outage must not flag every detector), **explicit zero-fill** (a dead detector must be a 0, not a missing row), and writing alert history only after the email sends. Gaps: **events 84–88 are ingested and never read**; a detector dead >7 days drops out of `known_detectors` and silently disappears; the CUSUM baseline is computed over the same 21 days that contain the failure; no holiday calendar (day-of-week term only); **no detector→phase/function table anywhere** (`detector_config=None`), so nothing there is circular for us.

## 2. What transferred — `src/health2.py`: within-sample, label-free, config-free, no history
`find_bad_intervals()` → `(DeviceId, Detector, start, end, reason)`, `Detector IS NULL` = whole signal. Reasons: `comm_loss` (no event of **any** allowed code for 300 s — the phase-colour codes must be in that test or a long coordinated green looks like an outage), `flash` (173 / 131 pattern 255), `fault_84..88`→83, `stuck_on` (one ON longer than `max(300 s, 3 × the signal's own median cycle)`), `chatter` (≥120 ONs in a clock minute, median ON < 0.5 s), `flatline` (silence while the **rest of the signal** implies ≥30 actuations — a demand-normalised peer test, so 03:00 is not an outage), `level_low/high` (hour-of-day vs the detector's own other days), and **`geh_anomaly`** = ODOT's method with the static median taken over the 3-day sample and the peer group = this signal's other channels. 375 DEV signals × 72 h in **189 s** (DuckDB 6 GB / 6 threads) → 53,252 intervals: stuck_on 4,656 (2,314 h), comm_loss 5,157, fault_88 40,306, fault_87 822, geh_anomaly 881, flatline 421, level_low 420, chatter 0.
**Validated against real production output** — `ATSPM_Report/tests/data/detector_health.parquet` (185 detectors × 21 days of ODOT's own `anomaly` booleans): re-deriving flags from a **3-day** slice gives **precision 0.62, recall 0.096** (mean of 19 slices). The port is right but deaf: without 5 weeks of history it sees ~1 in 10 of what production sees.

## 3. Historical health for 2024-12-02..04 does not exist
Production `detector_health` is `CREATE OR REPLACE`d each run, keeps 21 days (now 2026-08-31→09-20) and never stores the GEH score, only the boolean; Azure's own copy is on the repo's `NEVER_BACKFILL` list (outdated atspm). The only route is to re-derive from Azure `actuations` for the 35 days ending 2024-12-05, which is what `health_pull.py` does. **I did not connect** (shared-login rule); it imports `staging_auth.get_connection` from `src/download/`. For the user: `python src/download/health_pull.py probe`, then `... actuations --start 2024-10-31 --end 2024-12-05`, then `... rebuild --start 2024-12-02 --end 2024-12-05`. The script prints the caveat: if Azure's `actuations` lacks `fill_in_missing`, silent detectors are *absent* rather than 0 and the rebuild under-flags exactly the failures we want.

Two controls make this readable. The **rebuild control** shows the pipeline is bit-exact (features for untouched detectors are byte-identical, verified column by column), so no jitter is hiding anything. The **placebo** removes the same detectors' same durations at random times and costs −0.16 pt — so deleting an equivalent slice of *real* data does hurt slightly, while deleting the *flagged* periods costs nothing: the flagged periods are genuinely uninformative, but they were not harmful either. Note the base arm's own level moves .9068/.9127/.9146 across the three runs purely because `align()` leaves a different row set to train on — a ±0.4 pt swing, i.e. **an order of magnitude larger than any masking effect** (cf. stage 08's ±0.2 pt lesson). Masking is small *by construction*: a stuck ON is **one** actuation, a flatline has none, faults last seconds — only **174 of 4,873 labelled DEV detectors lose >1 % of their ON events** (1,058 lose >1 % of occupied *time*).

## 5. Recommendation
1. **Ship the detector, not the mask.** `find_bad_intervals` is cheap (189 s for 375 signals), interpretable, and reproduces 62 % of ODOT's production flags from 3 days. Surface the periods to the user ("detector stuck on 02:10–05:40 — period ignored") and on the review list. **Leave masking OFF by default** (`mask_bad_periods=False`): it moves nothing overall and is slightly negative where it bites.
2. **Do not add a trust model.** The decoder's own top probability is already the best available ranking of "is this right"; every learned alternative, with or without health, is at or below it.
3. **One real change for `predict.py`: refuse on `phase_prob`, not on `n_actuations < 5`.** The min-5 rule answers 86.1 % of detector-windows at .9686; a plain `phase_prob ≥ 0.9` answers **89.3 % at .9892** — more coverage *and* 2 pt more accuracy (min-10: 77.3 % / .9718 vs .9932 at equal coverage). Of the 13,534 detector-windows min-5 refuses, **10,356 have `phase_prob ≥ 0.9` and are 98.3 % correct** — the rule discards confident, correct answers because the sample was short. Keep `n_actuations` as the "low evidence" note and `review_flag`; make the *refusal* `phase_prob < th` (th ≈ 0.7–0.9, user's coverage choice), optionally `AND n_act < 5`.
4. Health belongs in the **status text and the review list**, not in the features or the score.
5. ODOT-grade health needs ODOT's input: ≥5 weeks of 15-min counts (`health_pull.py`), not a cleverer rule on 3 days.
6. **`data/detector_plans.parquet` (35,818 rows / 945 signals) carries the controller's own diagnostic settings** — and they are mostly *off*: `no_activity` is 0 on **every** row, `max_presence` is set on 618 rows, `erratic_count` on 1,013, `fail_time` on 2,303. That is exactly why our logs contain only faults 87/88 (hardware open/short) and never 84/85/86 (the diagnostic-driven codes). Where they *are* set they should replace our generic thresholds per detector (`max_presence` → `stuck_on_s`, `erratic_count`/`fail_time` → chatter), but with 2–6 % coverage a label-free default is still needed. Two cautions: the same table's `call_phase` is the label, so the file must never reach a model input; and `delay`/`extend` (set on 1,447 / 5,003 rows) change the *observed* ON/OFF timing, which both health thresholds and the function model see.

**Negatives / limits.** Masking was measured on the ranker, not the decoder (deliberate) and not on the 5 min / 10 min windows. `mask@both` differences (e.g. all .9127→.9159) are a retrain, so they carry the same run-to-run noise the untouched-slice row quantifies. `chatter` never fires on this data (threshold 120 ONs/min), `level_*` needs ≥2 days, and `geh_anomaly` needs ≥ a few hours to have a median worth differencing — below ~1 h the module degrades to faults + stuck-on + flash only. The flatline peer test assumes a detector's share of signal traffic is roughly stationary; a turn-lane detector during a demand shift could in principle trip it.
